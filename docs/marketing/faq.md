# Answers to the questions the posts will meet

**How is this different from Pixel Agents (or other tools that show agents as characters)?**
They show your agents, and do it well. Orkline runs the work: tasks come in from GitHub or a board, go down a line
of buildings, stop at a review gate, and leave as pull requests. The game is spending less per accepted result.

**Why orks?**
Because a camp of busy orks hauling work around is more fun to watch after a day of work than a dashboard, and it
shows the flow at a glance. If you'd rather it looked like work, switch to Office: the same town, calm and monochrome.

**Is it safe to let agents work on my repository?**
Nothing leaves the town without your nod at the Review gate. Work happens on branches; nothing is merged without you.
The Warder blocks risky commands. The first mission is read-only until you say otherwise.

**Does it use my subscription? Is that allowed?**
It runs your own, unmodified Claude Code and Codex, signed in with your own accounts, the way you'd run them in a
terminal. It never touches your sign-in. Agent work at night is off by default.

**Do I need API keys? Is there a cloud?**
No and no. It is local-first and open source (Apache 2.0).

**Which systems?**
Checked on macOS. Linux and Windows should work, not yet checked: tell us what breaks.

**Is the art AI-generated?**
The camp you see in early posts is concept art, made with an image model as a reference. The art that ships is
being hand-drawn by a pixel artist, credited, under an open licence.

**How much does it cost to run?**
What your own subscriptions cost. The camp shows how much of your limit each finished result used, and you can cap
what it may spend while you're away.
