# Design — name candidates, should Orkcraft be renamed

Status: candidates 2026-10-13, for the decision of [open-questions.md](open-questions.md) Q18 by Friday 17 October.
**Nothing here is checked:** each name is checked by the owner in the registers (USPTO, EUIPO, WIPO; classes 9 and
42), for a domain, a GitHub organisation, a PyPI name and handles. Not legal advice.

## 1. The criteria

1. **Neither "ork" nor "-craft"** (Q18), and **no word owned by the big fantasy and strategy games:** no Horde (a
   Blizzard faction), Grunt (their unit, and an existing JavaScript tool), Warband, Warcraft-like compounds.
2. **Says the camp and the factory,** not only one: the person's agents as a camp that makes things.
3. **A coined compound, not a plain word:** a made-up pair is easier to own as a mark and to find in search than a
   dictionary word (Stockade, Warpath, Bonfire are taken many times over). The words people search ("Claude Code",
   "coding agents") go in the line under the name, not in it.
4. **Said once, spelt right:** a person who hears it on a call types it correctly.
5. **The orks stay inside** under any name: the characters are a common fantasy noun, the risk is in the product's
   name.

## 3. The first three, as a product view

1. **Tuskworks:** the strongest: the ork without the word, the factory in "works", short, a coined pair, a logo
   writes itself (a tusk as a gear's tooth).
2. **Clanyard:** best for the community story (the owner's second goal): "join the clan".
3. **Haulcamp:** closest to how the product works: carts on roads, a camp.

## 4. The quick checks, ten minutes a name

1. The registers, classes 9 and 42: the exact name and close spellings.
2. `<name>.dev` and `<name>.app`; `github.com/<name>`; `pypi.org/project/<name>`; the name on X, Bluesky and Discord.
3. A web search for "<name> software" and "<name> app".
4. Said aloud to two people, typed by them.

A name passing all four goes to the lawyer's hour beside "Orkcraft".

## 5. What a rename touches (an afternoon now)

The repository and organisation (GitHub redirects the old links), `pyproject.toml` and the command, the README, the
landing page and its domain, the logo's wordmark (the Almendra wordmark, color-pass §5), `updates.json` and the
installer's URLs. **Untouched:** the orks, the town, every code name and setting (CLAUDE.md: code keeps its names), the
interface's words.

## 6. The owner's candidate: OrkMine (2026-10-13)

Fits the rails and mine carts (color-pass §6d) and keeps the brand's "ork". Weighed against §1:

| for | against |
|---|---|
| **drops "-craft"**, the main weight of Q18 (Microsoft's family of marks reaching class 42) | **keeps "ork"**: if Games Workshop's ORK is registered in class 9, the name carries it; the lawyer's hour must clear ORK first |
| the factory said by the picture: a mine, carts on rails, work brought up | **"mine" reads as crypto mining** to many developers ("an ork mine" ≈ a mining pool): a first impression to test, not to argue |
| short, said once and spelt right | "Mine" also echoes Minecraft (the same owner as Warcraft), though far less than "-craft" |
| keeps the continuity: orks, the town, the domain's first letters | "The Mine" was the Research building's old Camp name (`lexicon.py` `was`); harmless, the word today is Research |

**Product view:** better than Orkcraft (the larger risk goes), not as safe as a name with no "ork". It stands or falls
on two checks this week: **the lawyer on ORK**, and **five developers asked "what does OrkMine do?"**: if two or more
say crypto, it is out.
